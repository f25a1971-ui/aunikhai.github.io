The Bioscience Challenge: Optimizing Cattle Growth

In Malaysian beef production, local indigenous Kedah-Kelantan (KK) cattle are highly valued for their hardiness and climate adaptability, but show slower relative growth rates compared to temperate breeds. To achieve cost-effective production, livestock technicians must identify the Point of Inflection ($f''(t) = 0$)—the precise age when daily weight gain reaches its absolute peak ($f'(t)$ is maximized). Beyond this point, feed conversion efficiency decreases, signalling the transition from muscle accumulation to lipid deposition.

Biological Growth Equations & Calculus Proof

The mathematical trajectory of animal growth follows a sigmoidal pattern modeled using the non-linear Gompertz Growth Function.

Primary Liveweight Equation:
$$f(t) = W(t) = A \cdot e^{-B \cdot e^{-k \cdot t}}$$
First Derivative (Instantaneous Growth Rate):
$$f'(t) = \frac{dW}{dt} = A \cdot B \cdot k \cdot e^{-k \cdot t} \cdot e^{-B \cdot e^{-k \cdot t}} = W(t) \cdot B \cdot k \cdot e^{-k \cdot t}$$

Variable Definition in Livestock Bioscience

  • $t$: Age of the animal, measured in months from birth.
  • $A$: Asymptote representing the mature liveweight (kg) that the animal achieves when aging to infinity.
  • $B$: Constant of integration related to birth weight (at $t = 0$, $W_0 = A e^{-B}$).
  • $k$: Maturing rate index per month—the speed at which the animal reaches its mature size limit.
Mathematical Verification of the Point of Inflection (Peak Growth Rate)

To locate the peak growth velocity point, we find the second derivative $f''(t)$ and set it equal to zero: $$f''(t) = \frac{d^2W}{dt^2} = k \cdot f'(t) \cdot \left(B e^{-kt} - 1\right) = 0$$ Since $k \neq 0$ and $f'(t) \neq 0$, the inflection occurs exactly when: $$B e^{-kt} = 1 \implies -kt = \ln\left(\frac{1}{B}\right) \implies t_{\text{inflection}} = \frac{\ln(B)}{k}$$ Substituting $t_{\text{inflection}}$ back into the primary weight equation yields the biological height at inflection: $$W(t_{\text{inflection}}) = A \cdot e^{-1} \approx 0.368 \cdot A$$ Conclusion: Kedah-Kelantan beef cattle hit their maximum growth rate precisely when they achieve 36.8% of their final adult weight.

Derivative Applications

Optimal Weaning Target

Weaning calves at 6–8 months guarantees high growth rate velocity ($f'(t) > 6$ kg/mo), easing transition onto mixed grazing.

Slaughter Optimization

Selling beef cattle between 18–24 months captures standard carcass efficiency before growth deceleration rate ($f''(t) < 0$) decreases profitability.

Breeding Replacement Selection

Cattle exhibiting a larger maturing index ($k$) reach target mature dimensions faster, reducing total herd emissions footprint.
